### 2.2 Why a single's song fails to match the album copy today (the core of item 3)

Two different matchers decide `TopSongRow.library`, and both miss it for separate reasons:

**Aurral rows** (`libraryCopies(of:)` in `AppModel+Trending.swift` → `DiscoverRules.mainCopy`):
1. The title is compared with `DiscoverRules.normalised` — case, accents and spaces only. Deezer's "Song (Radio Edit)",
   "Song - Single Version", "Song (feat. X)" never equal the library's "Song".
2. The Navidrome lookup is `search3(<Deezer title>)`; Navidrome needs every word, so "Song Radio Edit" never finds "Song".
3. Durations must be within **3 s**: a radio edit (3:31) and the album version (4:05) are 34 s apart.
4. **Exactly one** text match is required: an album copy plus a deluxe-edition or compilation copy of the same
   recording (both within 3 s) is "ambiguous" and returns nothing.
5. Misses are remembered for the session (`chartLookups`), so a song downloaded mid-session stays unmatched.

**ListenBrainz rows** (`librarySong(for:)` → `MatchIndex.librarySong`, the ladder in `MatchIndex.swift`):
1. Tier 1, recording MBID: ListenBrainz's top recording is often the **single's recording**. MusicBrainz keeps a
   radio edit or single mix as a separate recording from the album track, and the album file Lidarr tagged carries the
   album track's recording MBID. Different ids, no match.
2. Tiers 3–4 (artist + title + ≤ 3 s): `Normalize.name` does fold "Radio Edit" / "Single Version" (it's in
   `sameRecordingPhrases`), but the **3 s window** fails for an edit.
3. Tier 5 (name only) applies only when a duration is **unknown**; ListenBrainz always sends `length`, so two known
   lengths more than 3 s apart never match by name (`match` returns nil before `librarySong`'s unique-name rule).

So the cause is the 3 s duration rule (both paths), the recording-MBID difference between single and album (ListenBrainz),
and the bare-fold title comparison, whole-title search and uniqueness rule (Aurral). Relaxing those globally is not
safe: the same rules decide "in library" for charts, Discover and Try (D100, D104a M6, D155).

### 2.3 The sources (verified in their source code)

- **Aurral 2.13** `GET /api/artists/:mbid/preview` (`backend/routes/artists/handlers/preview.ts`, tag v2.13.0): resolves

### 2.4 The method chosen for item 3, and why it is the most efficient safe one

**Lidarr's own tracklist of the artist, matched by a strict title rule, then linked to the library.** The artist is in
Lidarr by definition, and Lidarr already holds MusicBrainz's tracklists for exactly this artist's releases, with each
release's type and which tracks have a file. Two GETs on the owner's network replace dozens of rate-limited MusicBrainz
calls, and the release types let Magpie refuse live, remix and demo releases outright — something a library-only
title match can't do (e.g. "Alive 2007" holds live takes under plain titles). Regex alone is not enough; the strict
title rule (D261) does the text part. MusicBrainz is not used this round (section 9).

Cost per artist open (refresh): Aurral 1 GET (session-cached), ListenBrainz 1 GET, Navidrome 1 `getTopSongs`, Lidarr 2
GETs (only when an Aurral or ListenBrainz song needs them), at most 5 `getSong` (to show matched rows) and at most 5
`search3` (only for songs too new for the match index). **0 MusicBrainz, 0 writes, no new route.**
